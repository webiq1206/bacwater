# Plain-language explanations of product mechanisms

## Request and scope

The owner asked for substantive explanations of how every research product works and what researchers are trying to learn from it. The previous copy identified receivers or signals without explaining the next step. This release replaces all 50 mechanism explanations and improves their short summaries and research-purpose paragraphs. The exact partner names, aliases, IDs, destinations and WEBIQ attribution are preserved.

The source reviewed at the start of this task was main fc8522c3b94d8bdce0bf97afe548cd3a72d37b38. The current shared search and controlled product-detail dialogs are retained. No calculator formulas, session state, user records, dependency versions, lockfile, security checks, database schema or deployment configuration are changed.

## Editorial structure

Every mechanism contains two short paragraphs, 70 to 145 words in total. They explain a target or starting event, the action that follows, the research question, and a concrete evidence boundary. Examples include glucose-linked insulin release, the different role of glucagon in liver glucose output, cells gripping surfaces through attachment proteins, electron exchange between NAD+ and NADH, and peptide-triggered release of stored growth hormone. Terms are defined in context rather than replaced by vague references to cell signaling.

Two actual paragraphs are rendered in the shared lightbox. The existing internal scrolling, pinned affiliate action, nested-search behavior and focus return are retained. Sources can now include a note explaining what the reference does and does not support. Chemical background references are distinguished from experimental papers.

## Scientific boundaries

This is an explanation of compound research, not a promise that a merchant product produces a health outcome. It contains no administration instructions, recommended amounts, treatment recommendations, guaranteed benefits or fabricated supplier testing. Research-only and commission notices remain visible. A disclaimer is not used to excuse unsupported claims.

Particular distinctions reviewed:

- GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon do not all have the same effect on glucose. Retatrutide research compares their combined effects rather than counting targets as benefits.
- The BPC-157 description reports cultured rat tendon-cell attachment and movement findings. It does not claim that the peptide directly rebuilds tissue or that its first receptor is established.
- Full thymosin beta-4 evidence is not represented as proof for every TB-500 fragment. The exact peptide identity matters.
- The CJC-1295 albumin-binding study concerns a DAC-bearing form. Its duration is not assigned to the no-DAC blend.
- The SELANK cell study includes a condition in which SELANK alone did not change the measured gene expression. This is not described as proof of direct GABA receptor activation.
- IGF-1 LR3 comparisons depend on binding proteins. Less binding to those proteins is not the same as universally stronger receptor activity.
- SNAP-8 source notes identify the SNARE papers as background research, not direct validation of SNAP-8 or a cosmetic product.
- Adalank and Adamax use the partner's described molecular designs. Parent SELANK/SEMAX findings do not prove the modified compounds' stability, delivery or mechanism.
- The key 2014 Dihexa HGF/c-Met mechanism paper was retracted in 2025. The retraction is stated in the mechanism, research-purpose paragraph, limit and source note. It is not offered as supporting evidence. This does not claim that every paper about Dihexa was retracted.
- Blends and prepared solutions retain formulation-specific limits instead of inheriting unproved synergy or delivery claims from individual-compound research.
